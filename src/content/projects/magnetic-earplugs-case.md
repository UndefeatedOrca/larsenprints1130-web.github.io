## Goals

I got a pair of reusable earplugs for daily carry, but the case they came with was too small, bad for fidgeting with, and generally displeasing to me. I decided to take things into my own hands.

## Design

I wanted the case to be:

- A good fit for the earplugs without taking up too much space in my pocket.
- Durable for everyday use.
- 3D printed, so I could easily manufacture and iterate, along with the cool-factor of having something 3D printed on me at all times to show people.
- Good to fidget with, since I generally need that in my life.
- Secure in its closed state so that the plugs don't scatter across the ground.
- Cheap (made from stuff I already have)

In its final form, I think the case accomplishes all these goals and more.

![An image of the earplugs case with the earplugs in front of it](/images/projects/earplugs-case/plugs-out.webp)

## Build
### Base Parts
The final design used basic Black PLA for the 3D printed components. I used a high infill so the whole case has a satisfying heft to it, but it's ultimately plastic so it doesn't feel heavy in my pocket.

<model-viewer src="/3d-models/earplugs-case.glb" alt="3D model of case" camera-controls auto-rotate environment-image="neutral" shadow-intensity="1" orientation="0deg 270deg 0deg"></model-viewer>

### Hinge
The hinge portion connecting the top and base postions of the case is actually a pin from the mechanism that holds most watch bands on to the lugs of the watch itself. I had a bunch of these on hand, and they install permanently and invisibly thanks to spring-loaded tips on either end of the pin, so the whole thing goes together seamlessly and holds up very well over time.
### Closure Mechanism
The clasp is just two opposite-attraction cylindrical magnets I had on hand. At first, they were just friction fit into the plastic, but I ultimately had to superglue them in place.
### Fit and Finish
I planned to leave it at that, but I decided to take the opportunity given to me by my boss to laser-engrave the top and bottom of the case. The top is just a plain cross as a reminder of my faith, and the bottom has my initials and phone number in case I ever drop or forget the case somewhere.

![A bottom view of the case, displaying the engraving](/images/projects/earplugs-case/bottom-engrave.webp)

 This was my first time engraving plastic and it turned out so clean and impressed me so much that I used it on another project (actually for my internship this time) like 2 weeks later, which turned out even better.

## Testing and iteration

I went through 2 models and printed prototypes before achieving everything I wanted in V3.

### Version 1:
- I attempted to use 3D printer filament as the hinge between the two clasps, which I failed to tolerance correctly.
- Blockiness in the hinge failed to let the mechanism rotate correctly. Just bad foresight there.
### Version 2:
- I fixed the blocky shape in the back and refined the size and shapes of the cavities for the earplugs so they fit without rattling around as much.
- Around this time I realized that a watch band pin might fit in the spot correctly and I happened to be exactly correct with that theory.
- I installed the magnets into this one for the first time and they barely did anything. I went two sizes up in the assortment of magnets I had on hand and that did the trick.

I don't have pictures of either of these because I just immediately threw them out. Sorry not sorry.

## Outcome

I am very proud of this project. It's a very simple solution to a barely-existing problem, but that's exactly the sort of thing I love 3D printing for. I think I met all of my goals and got to learn a little bit about new tools in the process.

I carried the earplugs and the case I made for the rest of my internship. After several weeks of daily use, the only failure point is that the pin inside the hinge mechanism seems to have broken on one end. The hinge still works but I believe the orientation I have it in and the somewhat loose fit at either end of the hinge slot attributed to a much different load case than the pin is designed for, so if I start using the earplugs day-to-day again I'll probably make one last iteration to the case tightening up the tolerances back there. As of right now, I am more than content.

As I mentioned, I utimately had to superglue the magnets in place. They haven't budged since then, and they hold the case shut even when dropping it onto concrete from pocket height. Take that, Apple.
